## Learning and assessment requirements

- **1.1:** Classify applications by the capability they use. Explain that a list of use cases is not a guarantee of reliable performance. Treat named model versions and industry examples as edition context.
- **1.2:** Distinguish an abstract computation model from a physical computer; explain Dartmouth's role in the source history; compare definitions of intelligence and distinguish narrow task performance from broader intelligence claims. Preserve the source's different philosophical framings instead of presenting them as one settled definition.
- **1.3:** Trace symbolic knowledge/rules/reasoning, connectionist data/loss/learning, and behaviorist agent/environment/reward loops. Compare strengths and limitations using cases. Assess hybrid systems without implying that one real system belongs exclusively to one school.
- **1.4:** Explain why capabilities, computing resources, expectations, investment, and deployment limits affect progress. Avoid scoring uncertain historical dates as unqualified facts until the source conflicts are resolved.
- **1.5:** Use distinct scenarios for sample-selection bias, privacy, misinformation, and value conflicts. Connect dataset curation before training, transparent standards during training, and accountability after deployment. Do not collapse all risks into a generic ethics question.
- **1.6:** Apply critical questioning and algorithmic understanding to unfamiliar tools. Explain the source's visually reviewed representation diagram. Use a responsible application-planning exercise rather than trivia about recommended websites or minutes of daily study.

Pretests should diagnose these distinctions; post-tests should apply them; parent summatives must integrate all children. Determine item counts after decomposing the objectives, with greater depth for 1.2, 1.3, and 1.5. The six-lesson outline alone is insufficient evidence of assessment coverage.

## Source issues to retain

1. **History dates conflict internally.** Page 29 describes a first winter beginning in 1979, while the p.30 timeline labels 1974–1980. Record both rather than silently harmonizing them.
2. **Timeline statements need verification.** Page 30 contains dated claims about ELIZA, neural-network methods, Stable Diffusion, and other milestones. They should not become confidently graded factual questions merely because they appear on a slide.
3. **Intelligence terminology is mixed.** Page 21 groups strong AI, human-level capability, and superintelligence in a way that needs an edition-context explanation. Avoid treating consciousness, general capability, and superior task performance as interchangeable.
4. **Multiple-intelligences table needs care.** The source's intrapersonal row also discusses naturalistic cognition. Preserve this as its presentation rather than silently representing it as an uncontested canonical taxonomy.
5. **Turing-test interpretation needs qualification.** Page 25 describes a reported chatbot result and a threshold, then gives the Chinese Room critique. A performance claim in a conversation test does not establish consciousness or genuine understanding.
6. **Ethical prompts are discussion questions.** Page 45 asks open normative questions. Notes and assessments should evaluate identification of trade-offs and source principles, not declare one universal answer to a moral dilemma.
7. **Governance examples are instructional context.** References to international organizations, legal accountability, and strategic policies are not current legal advice. The Three Laws of Robotics on p.39 are an illustrative framework, not enforceable technical guarantees.
